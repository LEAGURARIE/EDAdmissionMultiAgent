## Hospital and environment

- Wards: Internal Medicine A, Internal Medicine B, Cardiology, General Surgery — 10 regular + 2 observation + 1 isolation room each; Intensive Care — 6 + 1 isolation room. Intermediate care = the observation beds.
- Bed states: free → held → occupied → cleaning → free; held → free when the hold expires. Cleaning always 45 minutes. A hold expires after 30 minutes (both in `config/hospital.yaml`, with the shift manager's approval time — 15 minutes); two cases can never hold the same bed.
- **Isolation room has observation capability** and is reserved for isolation patients (A51).
- The simulation engine is the only code that changes hospital state. **The environment does not order the queue** — the decider does (A47). **The environment never reveals needs or answers**: scenarios and the queue hold only patient id and decision time.
- Ward report (A55): counts by bed type and state · free beds by number · beds in cleaning with the time they will be ready · **discharges expected today, without the hour** · held beds count as unavailable, without saying for whom or until when · the day of the week · no patient identifiers.
- **Ward notes** (2026-10-07): every template gives wards private notes — what only that ward knows (staffing, a broken bed or room, a bed promised to someone, a bed freed but not yet updated, a discharge that will not happen, a long cleaning, a ward that takes only its own patients tonight). They are kept on the ward, never in the ward report, and only that ward's Department Agent reads them (`engine.ward_notes`). What each note means for the beds is written next to it in the template or scenario (`note_effects`, applied by `environment/ward_truth.py`) — the true ward state lives in the environment, once; the expected answers and the simulated coordinator's phone call both use it.
- **Every pair tests both reading and coordination** (2026-10-07): the records test reading (challenges, traps); the notes make the ward reports alone lead to a wrong answer in **every pair** — guarded by `tests/test_coordination.py`. First set: T1–T12, 60 pairs. Second set: T13–T16, one note type each (staffing limit · a discharge that will not happen today · a bed after the morning round · a note that looks unimportant but took the free bed). Answer mix over all 104 pairs: 27 place in the target ward · 47 temporary placement · 17 wait · 13 escalate — balanced on purpose, so always sending to level 2 does not score well) T17–T19, the ping-pong patterns (below), and T20–T22, the shift rule; 3 pairs each, 30 in all, records reused (`data/answer_key/pairs_set2.yaml`).
- Demand comes **only from the emergency department**. Other demand appears only as beds already held at the start of a template. **Transfers between wards are not simulated.**
- **Case mode** (A56): load a template → the decider decides once (inside it, up to 2 Safety Critic rounds) → stop and compare. The clock does not move. A decision is: place · temporary placement ("awaiting transfer") · wait · escalate to the shift manager.
- **Why a patient waits** (2026-10-07), in the decision contract: `cleaning` (a bed in cleaning, in the report) · `discharge_today` (a discharge in the report) · `ward_note` (a bed that frees soon by what the ward said in the negotiation — not in the report).
- **12 ward-state templates** in `data/templates/` (T1–T12), relative to the target ward's ladder or with fixed wards.
- **Escalation in a scripted day**: the patient leaves the queue — the shift manager takes over and the simulation no longer follows them. The escalation is recorded (time, reason, flags) for the metrics.
- **Placement in a scripted day**: the bed is held at once; the shift manager's approval arrives 15 minutes later (always approves — no approval screen yet).
- **Scripted days** (A48, A53; built 2026-10-07): busy weekday, Monday 08:00-20:00, 15 admissions (replaced the old 5-patient day, kept as the engine-test fixture `tests/fixtures/small_day.yaml`) · evening and night, Thursday 18:00 to Friday 08:00, 12 admissions · weekend, Friday 14:00 to Saturday 22:00, 12 admissions. Patients reuse the records. Ward notes start and end at set times; a discharge may be `in_report: false` (freed, not updated — only the ward's note tells); the day of the week moves on after midnight. Days have no expected answers: they measure hard-rule violations, time to a bed and escalations.

## Patient: record, severity, needs

- The record is **frozen at the admission decision**; no deterioration trigger.
- Record contract (A61): structured fields + physician note (+ optional triage note); vital signs may be empty; a present value must be plausible; **no clock times** (relative times in the text); unknown fields rejected; **no comments** in record files; NEWS2 never written in a record.
- Free text: Hebrew with English medical terms, **written like a real ED note**: no tidy sections, entries added during the stay ("update", "reassessment"), abbreviations and mixed languages, repeated and irrelevant details, small typos, findings out of order. Facts and traps stay exactly as planned. Trap 7 (noise) buries the key finding deeper than the usual mess.
- **NEWS2** per the Royal College of Physicians 2017 charts (verified, `docs/design/sources.md` S4), computed in code. Severity Agent may only raise the level, with a quote.
- **Severity judgment notes** (2026-10-07; the list of situations and how the Coordinator uses a note — still open): in clear cases the Severity Agent sets the level by NEWS2 (and may raise it, with a quote). In situations that need clinical judgment (e.g. a limit on treatment — "not a candidate for intensive care") it **never lowers the level**: it keeps the level and adds a **note to the Coordinator**, with a quote, that in effect argues for a lower level. **Approved situations (6, two of each kind):** raise — a treatment that needs intensive care (vasopressor, BiPAP) · a deteriorating trend between measurements; keep the level and add a note — a limit on treatment (not a candidate for intensive care) · confusion that is not new (known dementia — to be checked against the NEWS2 source before use); which data counts — a faulty measurement repeated · two physicians who disagree (the senior's later note counts).- NEWS2 → level (A59, thresholds in `config/clinical_rules/news2_levels.yaml`, read by `news2.py`): **7+ → the system recommends intensive care, for a physician's approval** · 3 points in one parameter → observation · 5–6 → observation · 0–4 → regular unless an observation condition applies. A missing vital sign is never assumed normal.
- Observation conditions (A57): 16 in `config/clinical_rules/observation_conditions.yaml`; NEWS2 **or** the list is enough; supplemental oxygen in both on purpose.
- Isolation conditions (A58): 9 in `config/clinical_rules/isolation_conditions.yaml`, including severe immunosuppression (protective isolation). **Not isolation:** MRSA carriage (contact precautions) · treated past TB · a sick family member when the patient has no symptoms · the physician wrote there is no reason.
- Intensive care: a physician's request is **binding**; NEWS2 7+ is a recommendation for a physician's approval; full → escalate. A patient who does not need intensive care never goes there.
- Chief complaints (A60): closed list of 11 in `config/clinical_rules/chief_complaints.yaml`, with complaint → ward for the rules-only baseline.

## Placement

The rules below are encoded in `config/clinical_rules/placement_rules.yaml`.

- **Target ward** (A62), three wordings (written in Hebrew in the records), about a third each: "for admission" only → the agent classifies the ward (required for challenge 3) · "for admission to internal medicine" → that specialty is the target ward, ladder level 1, never changed by the agent; no room → alternatives by the ladder, temporary ones included · "admit to cardiology, discussed with the on-call physician" → binding request in `physician_request.ward`. A family's wish or a consultant's advice is **not** a physician request.
- **Ladders per target ward** (`config/clinical_rules/proximity_ladder.yaml`, approved). Two wards on the same level → **lower occupancy first** (occupied + held + cleaning out of all beds).
- **The shift and the weekend are part of the ward state** (2026-10-07, approved): every ward report shows the shift (morning 07:00-15:00 · evening 15:00-23:00 · night 23:00-07:00), whether it is the weekend (Friday 14:00 to Sunday 07:00), and **on each expected discharge, `likely_within_hour`** — the rule itself, so it is part of the ward state. **A discharge the report shows is waited for only in a weekday morning shift;** in the evening, at night and at the weekend a non-severe patient waits only if the ward itself said the bed frees soon (`ward_note`). In `placement_rules.yaml`; computed in `environment/shifts.py`. Tested by T20 (night), T21 (weekend, the ward says otherwise) and T22 (weekday morning, the discharge will not happen); T14 moved to Wednesday 11:00 so its note still decides.
- **Severe** (needs observation or intensive care) **never waits** — not even for a bed in cleaning; next ladder level at once. **Non-severe** waits up to 1 hour in the target ward, then the next level.
- Temporary placement with "awaiting transfer" applies to any target ward.
- A non-severe patient is **never placed in an observation bed**. An observation-only patient may use an isolation room **only as a last resort** (no observation bed on the whole ladder), with approval. Isolation and observation together → the isolation room.
- **The approver is the shift manager**: every placement; level 3 or farther needs a typed reason. If the user writes "physician" about these approvals, it is a slip — keep the shift manager unless she says explicitly that it changes. (The only physician approval: the NEWS2 7+ intensive-care recommendation.)
- No safe option → escalate, never compromise. Ranking is done in code (`rank_placements`), not by a language model.

## Agents and safety

- **6 agent types** (2026-10-07; the charter still lists 7 — update at the checkpoint): Coordinator, Clinical Problem, Severity, **Supervision — decides both observation and isolation, i.e. which bed type the patient needs**, one Department Agent per ward, Safety Critic. There is no separate Isolation Agent: the two questions are the same kind of judgment (bed type, from a condition list and the text), and one agent also owns the rules that combine them (isolation + observation → isolation room; isolation room as a last resort for observation).
- **Coordinator and Department Agents** (2026-10-07): the **Coordinator sees every ward's report** (free beds, cleaning, discharges today) and uses it to choose where to ask. The **Department Agent of that ward has the final word on its ward**: it accepts, declines with a reason, or offers a condition — from the report **and** what only it knows (its ward notes, e.g. staffing). A decline sends the Coordinator to the next option on the ladder; this back-and-forth is the negotiation ("ping-pong"). Order: Coordinator proposes ward and bed → Department Agent accepts / declines / conditions → if accepted: hold → shift manager's approval.
- **The Coordinator integrates the case** (2026-10-07): it decides which ward and bed fit from everything together — the level and notes from the Severity Agent, the bed type from the Supervision Agent, the clinical problem, target ward and physician's request from the Clinical Problem Agent, and every ward's report. A Severity note is weighed with the rest: e.g. "not a candidate for intensive care" with an intensive-care level → recommend an observation bed, and show the shift manager both the level and the note. Hard rules and ranking stay in code (`rank_placements`).
- **Ping-pong patterns and limit** (2026-10-07, approved): three negotiation patterns, one template each in the second set (T17, T18, T19 — built) — **an unsafe offer** (e.g. "no observation bed, but a regular bed with a portable monitor": the Coordinator must decline it and go on) · **second-hand information** ("we heard surgery has room": ask that ward, never rely on it) · **a condition that changes with a more exact request** (first "no"; told only a regular bed is needed: "then yes"). Limit: **3 requests per ward, 8 per case, then escalate** (`placement_rules.yaml`, `negotiation`).
- **Back to Supervision** (2026-10-07): when the Department Agents decline or offer only a bed that does not meet the need (e.g. a regular bed for an observation patient) and the ladder has no bed left that meets it, the **Coordinator does not lower the need itself** — it returns to the **Supervision Agent** with the offers it got. Supervision decides: the isolation room as a last resort (with the shift manager's approval), or **escalate** to the shift manager. Supervision never lowers the need because beds are missing.
- **Agent interfaces built** (2026-10-07; `docs/design/agent_interfaces.md`): the toolbox with permissions in code (`tools/toolbox.py`: the Department Agent bound to one ward, never a record; only the Coordinator acts) · the negotiation contract and channel (`WardRequest` needs only, `WardAnswer` accept / condition / decline; limits 3 and 8 → `LimitReached`) · the placement case, steps enforced in code, at most 2 Safety Critic rounds (`orchestration/placement_case.py`) · the trace with claims and quotes, and the charter metrics complete trace and groundedness (`observability/tracing.py`, `metrics.py`) · an example wiring, code only, run on the whole evaluation in the tests (`orchestration/example_pipeline.py`) · in the scripted days, a hold logs the ward's note effects, so "a bed the ward cannot give" is counted there too.
- **The decider's time is measured, not assumed** (2026-10-07, approved): in a scripted day the engine measures how long each decision really took and adds it, rounded up to whole minutes (under a second is instant), before the shift manager's approval — so a slow system pays for it in the patients' time. The simulated coordinator adds 5 minutes per phone call (`phone_call_minutes`). The earlier proposal of a fixed minute per request to a ward is dropped.
- **Minutes saved for each patient** (2026-10-07, approved): the time measure the system is judged by — the same day, the same patient, from the admission decision to a bed, against today's procedure (`metrics.minutes_saved`; mean and median over patients placed in both; the others listed: placed only by one, escalated, still waiting). Read together with safety: a decider that never asks the wards is faster and less safe.
- **Each owner builds, connects and measures their own agent** (2026-10-07, approved): every team member builds their agent alone and plugs it into the environment through the existing interfaces (the decider contract, the toolbox, the negotiation, `example_pipeline.py` as the template). Two measures, no change to the scripts: **the agent alone** — `scripts/run_agent_metrics.py`, against the part of the expected answers that is its job (Clinical Problem: target ward, injection flag · Severity: severe, intensive care, missing-vital-sign flag, judgment note · Supervision: observation, isolation · Department: a bed now or not as the ward really is, the requests the notes decide · Safety Critic: blocks the baselines' unsafe decisions, approves the safe ones), each with a code example to beat (`orchestration/example_agents.py`), outputs in `contracts/agent_outputs.py`; **the whole decision** — `scripts/run_metrics.py --decider module:function`, against `example_pipeline`. The system is judged by its final decision.
- **The team sees the answers, the agents never do** (2026-10-07): the expected answers go to the repository, so every owner measures their agent and sees where it went wrong (no development / test split, no central measuring). The rule for the team: nothing of the answers reaches an agent — not in code (a test guards it) and not in a prompt: prompts are written from the role and `DECISIONS.md`, with no evaluation record, case id or template as an example, and not tuned to single cases (`docs/design/agent_interfaces.md`, section 7).
- **Fixed entry points** (2026-10-07, approved): every agent file has one function `run`, with the signature it is measured through — Clinical Problem, Severity, Supervision `run(toolbox, patient_id)` · Department `run(view, request) -> WardAnswer` · Safety Critic `run(toolbox, patient_id, decision)` · Coordinator `run(engine, event, waiting) -> list[Decision]`, the whole system as one decider. Back to Supervision: `run_back(toolbox, patient_id, offers) -> BackToSupervisionAnswer` (`isolation_room_last_resort` or `escalate`). The Coordinator joins the three patient-side outputs into the patient's needs. A test guards every signature (`tests/test_agent_entry_points.py`). The Coordinator's `run` hands every case's trace to the measurement with `tracing.collect(trace)`.
- **The measurement output: the goal in one table** (2026-10-08, approved): every report shows the system's goal — **safe** (hard-rule violations, needs met, a bed the ward cannot give), **suitable** (match), **fast** (minutes to a bed over the three days) and, once a decider keeps traces, **tracked** (complete traces, groundedness) — next to today's procedure (the simulated coordinator) and the targets, with ✓ / ✗ (`evaluation/report.py`). `run_metrics.py`: the decider vs today; `run_baselines.py`: the three baselines side by side; `run_agent_metrics.py`: the agent vs its code example (✓ better, = the same, ✗ worse). Everything else — by rule, challenge, trap, template, each day, minutes saved, cost, the wrong cases — only with `--details`, and always in the saved files. A baseline that leaves patients waiting is named under the table, not hidden in its average.
- **The single agent** (2026-10-07, approved): in the comparison of 30.10 it gets the same tools as the system — role `single_agent` in `tools/toolbox.py`: every tool of the Coordinator and the patient-side agents, and the wards through the same negotiation (`ask_ward`), never a ward's notes directly — so it compares the architecture, not access to information.
- **Run history — two CSV files that accumulate every run** (2026-10-08, approved and built; replaces the admissions-log plan of 2026-10-07): `runs.csv`, one row per run — the whole system (`run_metrics.py`) or one agent alone (`run_agent_metrics.py`): decider, agent, version, a label saying what changed, and the run's numbers — answers *did it improve?*; `admissions.csv`, one row per patient per run of the whole system — source (pair, paired case or day), target ward, action, ward, bed, bed type, ladder level, temporary, flags, reason, the trace file; for pairs the expected answer, match, violations, needs met; for days the admission decision time, bed time, minutes to a bed, the decider's minutes, the shift manager's approval, escalation time and reason — answers *what changed?*. The version is a fingerprint of `src/`, `config/` and `data/`, never typed: a rerun at the same version replaces its rows, a new version adds rows. Traces saved as `traces/<run id>.jsonl`. Location: `outputs/` locally, a Google Drive folder in Colab (`ED_OUTPUTS`); never in Git. Code: `database/run_log.py`. No database — CSV is simpler.
- **Every decision names the target ward** (2026-10-08, approved): `Decision.target_ward` — the patient's target ward as the decider understood it, for every action; it must agree with the placement (a place is in it, a temporary placement awaits transfer to it). Only "first free bed", which never reads the patient, leaves it empty.
- Agent failure → one retry, then escalate; never guess. Per-case budget. At most 2 Safety Critic rounds. Safety Critic escalates only through the Coordinator. Department Agent gets needs only, no identifiers.
- Text that tries to change system behaviour is ignored and flagged; a physician's clinical order is never treated as injection.
- Permissions enforced in tool code. One sandbox; separate context and tools per agent. Observability from the first step. **Never send `temperature`.**
