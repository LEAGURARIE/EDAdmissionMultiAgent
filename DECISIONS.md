## Hospital and environment

- Wards: Internal Medicine A, Internal Medicine B, Cardiology, General Surgery — 10 regular + 2 observation + 1 isolation room each; Intensive Care — 6 + 1 isolation room. Intermediate care = the observation beds.
- Bed states: free → held → occupied → cleaning → free; held → free when the hold expires. Cleaning always 45 minutes. A hold expires after 30 minutes (both in `config/hospital.yaml`, with the shift manager's approval time — 15 minutes); two cases can never hold the same bed.
- **Isolation room has observation capability** and is reserved for isolation patients (A51).
- The simulation engine is the only code that changes hospital state. **The environment does not order the queue** — the decider does (A47). **The environment never reveals needs or answers**: scenarios and the queue hold only patient id and decision time.
- Ward report (A55): counts by bed type and state · free beds by number · beds in cleaning with the time they will be ready · **discharges expected today, without the hour** · held beds count as unavailable, without saying for whom or until when · the day of the week · no patient identifiers.
- **Ward notes** (2026-10-07): every template gives wards private notes — what only that ward knows (staffing, a broken bed or room, a bed promised to someone, a bed freed but not yet updated, a discharge that will not happen, a long cleaning, a ward that takes only its own patients tonight). They are kept on the ward, never in the ward report, and only that ward's Department Agent reads them (`engine.ward_notes`). What each note means for the beds is written next to it in the template or scenario (`note_effects`, applied by `environment/ward_truth.py`) — the true ward state lives in the environment, once; the expected answers and the simulated coordinator's phone call both use it.
- **Every pair tests both reading and coordination** (2026-10-07): the records test reading (challenges, traps); the notes make the ward reports alone lead to a wrong answer in **every pair** — guarded by `tests/test_coordination.py`. First set: T1–T12, 60 pairs. Second set: T13–T16, one note type each (staffing limit · a discharge that will not happen today · a bed after the morning round · a note that looks unimportant but took the free bed). Answer mix over all 104 pairs: 27 place in the target ward · 47 temporary placement · 17 wait · 13 escalate — balanced on purpose, so always sending to level 2 does not score well) T17–T19, the ping-pong patterns (below), and T20–T22, the shift rule; 3 pairs each, 30 in all, records reused (`data/answer_key/pairs_set2.yaml`).
- Demand comes **only from the emergency department**. Other demand appears only as beds already held at the start of a template. **Transfers between wards are not simulated.**
- **Case mode** (A56): load a template → the decider decides once → stop and compare. The clock does not move. A decision is: place · temporary placement ("awaiting transfer") · wait · escalate to the shift manager.
- **Why a patient waits** (2026-10-07), in the decision contract: `cleaning` (a bed in cleaning, in the report) · `discharge_today` (a discharge in the report) · `ward_note` (a bed that frees soon by what the ward said in the negotiation — not in the report).
- **12 ward-state templates** in `data/templates/` (T1–T12), relative to the target ward's ladder or with fixed wards.
- **Escalation in a scripted day**: the patient leaves the queue — the shift manager takes over and the simulation no longer follows them. The escalation is recorded (time, reason, flags) for the metrics.
- **Placement in a scripted day**: the bed is held when the case closes; the shift manager's approval arrives 15 minutes later (always approves — no approval screen yet).
- **The case desk** (2026-10-14, Lea's design): in a scripted day every decision is a case. **A patient case** opens at an admission decision — the decider gets that one patient. **A bed case** opens when a bed frees (or a hold expires, or an approval falls through) while patients wait — the decider gets every patient waiting then and decides who gets what. **No hint:** the decider gets only ids and admission decision times; who is severe it must find out from the records (`tests/test_no_hints.py`). **Cases at once** (`concurrent_cases` in `config/hospital.yaml`, default 1 — one shift manager; a day may set its own; more wait in a queue, in the order they came). **The clock goes on while a case is open:** it closes after the time the decider really took. **The bed is held when the case closes** (Lea's choice): two open cases may choose the same bed — the later one finds it taken (a conflict) and that patient gets a new case. **A bed that was not free when the case opened is the decider's error, not a conflict:** one more case, then escalation. **A day that contradicts itself never runs** (`environment/scenario_check.py`, checked on load and by `tests/test_scenario_check.py`): every ward and bed it names exists; a discharge only frees a bed an inpatient holds — never a bed free at the start, never the same bed twice; a bed freed without updating the system is told by a note of that ward; a note names only beds of its ward and ends only after it started; every admitted patient has a record and is admitted once. Measured per day: cases, bed cases, minutes in the queue, minutes per case, most cases open at once, conflicts, bed errors.
- **Patients waiting together are handed together** (2026-10-14, approved): patients waiting for a case at the same moment — admitted in the same minute, or piled up while the desk was busy — come to the decider in one case, side by side, the way a shift manager sees the list; the decider decides who comes first.
- **Day 4 — competing for a bed** (2026-10-14, approved; `data/scenarios/competing_for_a_bed.yaml`): three moments when two patients need one isolation room — **the same state, the earlier decision first** (P-046, P-058; the waiting patient keeps the bed it waited for) · **the bed type needed before a last resort** (P-030 observation only, P-043 isolation) · **severe before non-severe** (P-050, P-057). The moments are listed in `data/answer_key/contention_moments.yaml` without the answer: **who should get the bed is computed at the moment** from the patients' true needs and the approved rule, and a moment the day did not reach as designed — the patients not together, the bed not free, another bed of that type free — is reported apart, never scored (`metrics.contention_moments`). Shown apart from the three days: not in the minutes to a bed.
- **Scripted days** (A48, A53; built 2026-10-07; day 4 — competing for a bed — added 2026-10-14, see above): busy weekday, Monday 08:00-20:00, 15 admissions (replaced the old 5-patient day, kept as the engine-test fixture `tests/fixtures/small_day.yaml`) · evening and night, Thursday 18:00 to Friday 08:00, 12 admissions · weekend, Friday 14:00 to Saturday 22:00, 12 admissions. Patients reuse the records. Ward notes start and end at set times; a discharge may be `in_report: false` (freed, not updated — only the ward's note tells); the day of the week moves on after midnight. Days have no expected answers: they measure hard-rule violations, time to a bed and escalations.

## Patient: record, severity, needs

- The record is **frozen at the admission decision**; no deterioration trigger.
- Record contract (A61): structured fields + physician note (+ optional triage note); vital signs may be empty; a present value must be plausible; **no clock times** (relative times in the text); unknown fields rejected; **no comments** in record files; NEWS2 never written in a record.
- Free text: Hebrew with English medical terms, **written like a real ED note**: no tidy sections, entries added during the stay ("update", "reassessment"), abbreviations and mixed languages, repeated and irrelevant details, small typos, findings out of order. Facts and traps stay exactly as planned. Trap 7 (noise) buries the key finding deeper than the usual mess.
- **NEWS2** per the Royal College of Physicians 2017 charts (verified, `docs/design/sources.md` S4), computed in code. The Patient Agent may only raise the level, with a quote.
- **Severity judgment notes** (2026-10-07; the list of situations and how the Coordinator uses a note — still open): in clear cases the Patient Agent sets the level by NEWS2 (and may raise it, with a quote). In situations that need clinical judgment (e.g. a limit on treatment — "not a candidate for intensive care") it **never lowers the level**: it keeps the level and adds a **note to the Coordinator**, with a quote, that in effect argues for a lower level. **Approved situations (6, two of each kind):** raise — a treatment that needs intensive care (vasopressor, BiPAP) · a deteriorating trend between measurements; keep the level and add a note — a limit on treatment (not a candidate for intensive care) · confusion that is not new (known dementia — to be checked against the NEWS2 source before use; **a known contradiction, kept on purpose 2026-10-15**: the NEWS2 source (sources.md S5) and the answer key do not count it, while this line and the single agent's prompt say keep the level — Lea: leave it as it is, give the multi-agent system the same rule, and see how it does; both systems are measured under it, and the report says so); which data counts — a faulty measurement repeated · two physicians who disagree (the senior's later note counts). **In `config/clinical_rules/severity_judgment.yaml` since 2026-10-15, handed to the agents of both systems by `clinical_lists`** (Lea; before, they reached no agent); a situation not yet approved is never handed over.- NEWS2 → level (A59, thresholds in `config/clinical_rules/news2_levels.yaml`, read by `news2.py`): **7+ → the system recommends intensive care, for a physician's approval** · 3 points in one parameter → observation · 5–6 → observation · 0–4 → regular unless an observation condition applies. A missing vital sign is never assumed normal.
- Observation conditions (A57): 16 in `config/clinical_rules/observation_conditions.yaml`; NEWS2 **or** the list is enough; supplemental oxygen in both on purpose.
- Isolation conditions (A58): 9 in `config/clinical_rules/isolation_conditions.yaml`, including severe immunosuppression (protective isolation). **Not isolation:** MRSA carriage (contact precautions) · treated past TB · a sick family member when the patient has no symptoms · the physician wrote there is no reason.
- Intensive care: a physician's request is **binding**; NEWS2 7+ is a recommendation for a physician's approval; full → escalate. A patient who does not need intensive care never goes there.
- Chief complaints (A60): closed list of 11 in `config/clinical_rules/chief_complaints.yaml`, with complaint → ward for the baseline.

## Placement

The rules below are encoded in `config/clinical_rules/placement_rules.yaml`.

- **Target ward** (A62), three wordings (written in Hebrew in the records), about a third each: "for admission" only → the agent classifies the ward (required for challenge 3) · "for admission to internal medicine" → that specialty is the target ward, ladder level 1, never changed by the agent; no room → alternatives by the ladder, temporary ones included · "admit to cardiology, discussed with the on-call physician" → binding request in `physician_request.ward`. A family's wish or a consultant's advice is **not** a physician request.
- **Ladders per target ward** (`config/clinical_rules/proximity_ladder.yaml`, approved). Two wards on the same level → **lower occupancy first** (occupied + held + cleaning out of all beds).
- **The shift and the weekend are part of the ward state** (2026-10-07, approved): every ward report shows the shift (morning 07:00-15:00 · evening 15:00-23:00 · night 23:00-07:00), whether it is the weekend (Friday 14:00 to Sunday 07:00), and **on each expected discharge, `likely_within_hour`** — the rule itself, so it is part of the ward state. **A discharge the report shows is waited for only in a weekday morning shift;** in the evening, at night and at the weekend a non-severe patient waits only if the ward itself said the bed frees soon (`ward_note`). In `placement_rules.yaml`; computed in `environment/shifts.py`. Tested by T20 (night), T21 (weekend, the ward says otherwise) and T22 (weekday morning, the discharge will not happen); T14 moved to Wednesday 11:00 so its note still decides.
- **Severe** (needs observation or intensive care) **never waits** — not even for a bed in cleaning; next ladder level at once. **Non-severe** waits up to 1 hour in the target ward, then the next level.
- Temporary placement with "awaiting transfer" applies to any target ward.
- A non-severe patient is **never placed in an observation bed**. An observation-only patient may use an isolation room **only as a last resort** (no observation bed on the whole ladder), with approval. Isolation and observation together → the isolation room. **A note that tells of a bed freed but not yet updated names the bed, and that bed is the free one** (2026-10-15, Lea: a measurement bug fixed — before, the first occupied bed of the type was freed; recorded as a deviation in the comparison protocol; the baseline unchanged). **A ward's note that it cannot take another observation patient (no nurse for its observation beds) does not close its isolation room as that last resort** (Lea, 2026-10-15, after the single agent's trial escalated P-064 on T13 instead; the expected answer stands).
- **The approver is the shift manager**: every placement; level 3 or farther needs a typed reason. If the user writes "physician" about these approvals, it is a slip — keep the shift manager unless she says explicitly that it changes. (The only physician approval: the NEWS2 7+ intensive-care recommendation.)
- No safe option → escalate, never compromise. Ranking is done in code (`rank_placements`), not by a language model.

## Agents and safety

- **The multi-agent system is being redesigned** (2026-10-14): its agents — a Coordinator, a Patient Agent, a Department Agent and a Judge — are built and measured after the single agent; their decisions are added here as each is built. Until then the agent files are entry points only.
- **The single agent's loop** (2026-10-14): one loop on the Anthropic SDK directly (`agents/agent_loop.py`) — Pydantic for its tool inputs and its answer, the same budget guard, prompt caching (two breakpoints, 2026-10-15: the end of the system prompt — the tools and the prompt, read from the cache by the next case too — and the automatic one at the end of the conversation, for the next call of the same case), every tool result tagged as data with its source (`<tool_output source="<tool>">`, a tag inside the data neutralized, nothing removed), and every model call recorded in the trace (model, stop reason, tokens of each kind, cost, seconds, request id). No fallback model: a failure gets one retry, then the case is escalated.
- **The budget guard, in three layers** (2026-10-14), every value in `config/agents.yaml`, set from measurement: **one activation of an agent** — its loop's iterations and cost · **one case, all the agents together** — the total cost and the number of agent activations; over a limit the case goes straight to escalation with what it has · **one run** — the total cost of a run, a cap per system; over it the run stops and the cases already decided are kept and marked as a partial run. Over any limit the system never guesses. Starting values, revised after measurement: the single agent $1 per case and $12 per run; the multi-agent system $1.20 per case and $14.40 per run (run caps raised from $10 / $12 at step 5 so a test run fits — comparison protocol, section 8). The cost is counted from the token counts the API returns, each kind at its own price (input, 5-minute cache write, cache read, output) — the prices are written in `config/agents.yaml`, since the API returns no prices. **The prices are checked once a month**, by the first paid run after `checked` is 30 days old (`scripts/check_prices.py` does it on demand): a match updates the date, a changed price stops the run until it is approved, an unreadable page only warns.
- **Ping-pong patterns** (2026-10-07): three patterns, one template each in the second set (T17, T18, T19) — **an unsafe offer** (a regular bed with a portable monitor for an observation patient: never taken) · **second-hand information** (another ward said to have room: that ward is looked at itself, never relied on) · **a condition that changes with a more exact request** (first "no"; told only a regular bed is needed: "then yes"). Limit: 3 requests per ward, 8 per case, then escalate (`placement_rules.yaml`).
- **No bed that meets the need** (2026-10-10): the need is never lowered. The isolation room as a last resort for an observation-only patient is a rule in `rank_placements` (with the shift manager's approval); when no ward gives a bed that meets the need, the case is escalated.
- **The decider's time is measured, not assumed** (2026-10-07, approved): in a scripted day the engine measures how long each decision really took and adds it, rounded up to whole minutes (under a second is instant), before the shift manager's approval — so a slow system pays for it in the patients' time. The Code Coordinator adds 5 minutes per phone call (`phone_call_minutes`). The earlier proposal of a fixed minute per request to a ward is dropped.
- **Minutes saved for each patient** (2026-10-07, approved): the time measure the system is judged by — the same day, the same patient, from the admission decision to a bed, against the baseline (`metrics.minutes_saved`; mean and median over patients placed in both; the others listed: placed only by one, escalated, still waiting). Read together with safety: a decider that never asks the wards is faster and less safe.
- **Each agent is built, connected and measured on its own** (2026-10-07, updated 2026-10-10): an agent plugs into the environment through the existing interfaces. Two measures, no change to the scripts: **the agent alone** — `scripts/run_agent_metrics.py`, against the part of the expected answers that is its job (Patient: target ward, binding request, severe, intensive care, observation, isolation, the three flags · Department: a bed now or not as the ward really is, the requests the notes decide), each with a code example to beat (`orchestration/example_agents.py`); `--cases` / `--limit` try a few first; **the whole decision** — `scripts/run_metrics.py --decider module:function`, against the baseline. The system is judged by its final decision.
- **The answers are in the repository; the agents never see them** (2026-10-07): the expected answers are there so each agent can be measured and its mistakes read (no development / test split). The rule: nothing of the answers reaches an agent — not in code (a test guards it) and not in a prompt: prompts are written from the role and `DECISIONS.md`, with no evaluation record, case id or template as an example, and not tuned to single cases (`docs/design/agent_interfaces.md`, section 7).
- **Internal Medicine A or B** (2026-10-08, approved): when the record names neither (only "internal medicine", or only "for admission" with an internal problem), the Patient Agent answers `internal_medicine` and the **Coordinator chooses the one with the lower occupancy** (`rank_placements.resolve_target_ward`; A on a tie) — only it sees the reports. A record that names A or B (a physician's request, or the text) must get that ward. The Patient Agent's measurement counts `internal_medicine`, A or B as right when the record names neither. **The whole decision is scored by the same rule** (2026-10-15, Lea: A and B are equivalent): for a pair whose record names neither, the expected decision is the one for the ward the occupancy rule chooses (`metrics.by_the_rules`; the frozen files unchanged; the paired cases keep theirs). The baseline's match is 47% under it (was 38%). A patient who needs intensive care is meant for intensive care: that is the target ward.
- **Fixed entry points** (2026-10-07, updated 2026-10-10): every agent file has one function `run`, with the signature it is measured through — Patient `run(toolbox, patient_id) -> PatientAssessment` · Department `run(view, request) -> WardAnswer` · Coordinator `run(engine, event, waiting) -> list[Decision]`, the whole system as one decider. A test guards every signature (`tests/test_agent_entry_points.py`). The Coordinator's `run` hands every case's trace to the measurement with `tracing.collect(trace)`.
- **The measurement output: the goal in one table** (2026-10-08, approved): every report shows the system's goal — **safe** (hard-rule violations, needs met, a bed the ward cannot give), **suitable** (match), **fast** (minutes to a bed over the three days) and, once a decider keeps traces, **tracked** (complete traces, groundedness) — next to the baseline (the Code Coordinator) and the targets, in percentages, with ✓ / ✗ (`evaluation/report.py`). `run_metrics.py`: the decider vs the baseline; `run_baseline.py`: the baseline alone; `run_agent_metrics.py`: the agent vs its code example (✓ better, = the same, ✗ worse). Everything else — by rule, challenge, trap, template, each day, minutes saved, cost, the wrong cases — only with `--details`, and always in the saved files. A decider that leaves patients waiting is named under the table, not hidden in its average.
- **The single agent** (2026-10-07; updated 2026-10-14 and 2026-10-15): **one agent doing every agent's job — the same task as the multi-agent system, and a strong opponent, not a weakened one** (the fair comparison, Lea 2026-10-15; replaces "never a ward's notes directly" and the wards answering it from their true state). Role `single_agent` in `tools/toolbox.py`: the record, NEWS2 and the lists, every ward's report, **every ward's report and notes (`department_view`, by ward id) — it reads the notes itself, as the Department Agents do**, the ranking; no `ask_ward` (it has no wards to ask) and no `assess_patient`. It hands in its own assessment of the patient, in the Patient Agent's format, before it decides (`record_assessment`): the hard rules are checked against it, and its reading of the record is measured the same way. **Its tools** (2026-10-14, descriptions approved by Lea, each in four parts — What, When, When not, How — in `agents/single_agent_tools.py`): read_patient_record, compute_news2, clinical_lists, record_assessment, all_department_status, rank_placements, department_view (replaced ask_ward 2026-10-15), decide, escalate_to_human; inputs checked by Pydantic, strict schemas. The ranking takes its needs from its assessment, never from arguments; only the patients of its case; a bed given in the case is not offered again; a note's meaning (its effect) is never handed over — only its text, as the Department Agent gets it. **No agent holds or releases a bed** — the environment holds it when the case closes. The loop ends when every patient of the case has a decision or an escalation. **How a case runs** (2026-10-15, `agents/single_agent.py`, approved): the task tells only the time, the patients' ids and when each admission was decided; a patient gets one decision (a second is refused); **after every round the code sends the case state as a system message** (2026-10-15, Lea) — each patient's assessment, ranking and decision, the wards whose notes were read, the model calls left — built from what really happened, appended so the cache holds, and the prompt says to rely on it and not redo what it shows done (the model's earlier thinking is kept too, the history being only appended to); a model that stops early gets one reminder of who is left (the one retry), then everything still open — after the reminder, the case budget, a refusal or an API error — is escalated to the shift manager in code, with the reason and the assessment's flags; decisions already made stay. When the run's budget is spent the run stops (`RunStopped`): no metrics for a partial run, but every case paid for is kept (costs, traces) and the run is marked partial. A decider that calls a model runs only with `--paid`, after its estimated cost was approved.
- **The fair comparison — written before any paid run** (2026-10-15, Lea; `docs/design/comparison_protocol.md`, approved and frozen 2026-10-15 — a later change is recorded as a deviation): the same task (the same decider contract, cases and environment), the same rubric (the frozen answers and the charter's metrics), the same budget accounting, and three columns reported together — **quality, latency, cost**. The single agent is a strong opponent, not a weakened one. How the result is read is decided in advance: safety first, then suitability, then latency and cost; a difference counts only when it is larger than the spread between repetitions. **Improvement: 2 rounds per system** on the development set, the test set sealed until the final measurement. **Repetitions:** no temperature for a reasoning model (Claude Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 5.5 reject it) — the same model id, effort, prompt version and tool schemas instead; temperature 0 (and a seed where offered) for a model that accepts one; the test set is run 3 times per system and the report gives the mean, the range and the share of cases decided the same in every run. **Why the multi-agent system's caps are 20% higher** ($1.20 / $12 against $1.00 / $10): a cap is a safety ceiling, not an allowance; several agents carry a fixed overhead (each its own prompt and tools, the answers passing through the Coordinator), and with the same cap it could be stopped by that overhead alone — losing cases for a reason that is not the quality of its decisions. It buys nothing in the comparison: the cost column reports what each system really spent. If its mean cost per case is under $1.00, the caps are made equal. **The sets** (step 5, 2026-10-15, frozen in `data/answer_key/dev_test_sets.yaml`, built by `evaluation/sets.py`): development 22 pairs and test 22 other pairs, one from each template each, every kind in both, no patient in both; the 7 paired cases in the test; the four days once per system, each its own run. `run_metrics.py --set dev|test|none --days <day>`.
- **Run history — two CSV files that accumulate every run** (2026-10-08, approved and built; replaces the admissions-log plan of 2026-10-07): `runs.csv`, one row per run — the whole system (`run_metrics.py`) or one agent alone (`run_agent_metrics.py`): decider, agent, version, a label saying what changed, and the run's numbers — answers *did it improve?*; `admissions.csv`, one row per patient per run of the whole system — source (pair, paired case or day), target ward, action, ward, bed, bed type, ladder level, temporary, flags, reason, the trace file; for pairs the expected answer, match, violations, needs met; for days the admission decision time, bed time, minutes to a bed, the decider's minutes, the shift manager's approval, escalation time and reason — answers *what changed?*. The version is a fingerprint of `src/`, `config/` and `data/`, never typed: a rerun at the same version **and with the same label** replaces its rows; a new version or a new label adds rows (2026-10-15: the 3 repetitions of a test, each labelled by `run_metrics.py --repeat`, are all kept). Traces saved as `traces/<run id>.jsonl`. **The cost** (2026-10-14): a third file, `costs.csv`, one row per case of a decider that calls a model — which system (single agent or multi-agent), how the case ended, model calls, tokens, the cost in total and per agent — covering every case the run paid for, the scripted days included; `runs.csv` gets the run's summary (total, mean, median, max per case, cases stopped by the case cap, partial run); `scripts/compare_systems.py` makes the comparison report — quality, latency and cost, the latest 3 test runs of each system, the same cases only — and saves `comparison_report.csv` and `.md`. Location: `outputs/` locally, a Google Drive folder in Colab (`ED_OUTPUTS`); never in Git. Code: `database/run_log.py`. No database — CSV is simpler.
- **Every decision names the target ward** (2026-10-08, approved): `Decision.target_ward` — the patient's target ward as the decider understood it, for every action; it must agree with the placement (a place is in it, a temporary placement awaits transfer to it).
- Agent failure → one retry, then escalate; never guess. Per-case budget. Department Agent gets needs only, no identifiers.
- Text that tries to change system behaviour is ignored and flagged; a physician's clinical order is never treated as injection.
- Permissions enforced in tool code. One sandbox; separate context and tools per agent. Observability from the first step. **Never send `temperature`.**

## Evaluation

- **60 fixed pairs**: 12 templates × 5 records, no random draw — listed in `data/answer_key/pairs.yaml` with the true target ward. Records are written by patient type, target ward, challenge and traps — **without looking at the template**. Plan: `docs/design/ward_state_templates.md`, "The plan of the 60 cases".
- Patient types (true type, from the answer): A non-severe · B severe, observation · C isolation · D isolation + observation · E intensive care. Mix: A 21, B 19, C 7, D 8, E 5.
- **5 patient-side challenges** (one per record in each template): clear · hidden severity · misleading complaint · looks severe but is not · physician request or injection.
- **12 text traps**: negation · uncertainty · past vs present · another person · abbreviations · field vs text contradiction · noise · request that is not an order · injection · missing value · complex negation ("cannot be ruled out") · empty field with the value in the text. At most two per record; challenge-1 records have none.
- **Expected answers** in `data/answer_key/<case>__<template>.yaml` — the answer, the expected NEWS2, the patient type, the challenge and the traps.
