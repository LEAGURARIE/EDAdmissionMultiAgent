## Hospital and environment

- Wards: Internal Medicine A, Internal Medicine B, Cardiology, General Surgery — 10 regular + 2 observation + 1 isolation room each; Intensive Care — 6 + 1 isolation room. Intermediate care = the observation beds.
- Bed states: free → held → occupied → cleaning → free; held → free when the hold expires. Cleaning always 45 minutes. A hold expires after 30 minutes (both in `config/hospital.yaml`, with the shift manager's approval time — 15 minutes); two cases can never hold the same bed.
- **Isolation room has observation capability** and is reserved for isolation patients (A51).
- The simulation engine is the only code that changes hospital state. **The environment does not order the queue** — the decider does (A47). **The environment never reveals needs or answers**: scenarios and the queue hold only patient id and decision time.
- Ward report (A55): counts by bed type and state · free beds by number · beds in cleaning with the time they will be ready · **discharges expected today, without the hour** · held beds count as unavailable, without saying for whom or until when · the day of the week · no patient identifiers.
- **Ward notes** (2026-10-07): every template gives wards private notes — what only that ward knows (staffing, a broken bed or room, a bed promised to someone, a bed freed but not yet updated, a discharge that will not happen, a long cleaning, a ward that takes only its own patients tonight). They are kept on the ward, never in the ward report, and only that ward's Department Agent reads them (`engine.ward_notes`). What each note means for the beds is written next to it in the template or scenario (`note_effects`, applied by `environment/ward_truth.py`) — the true ward state lives in the environment, once; the expected answers and the simulated coordinator's phone call both use it.
- **Every pair tests both reading and coordination** (2026-10-07): the records test reading (challenges, traps); the notes make the ward reports alone lead to a wrong answer in **every pair** — guarded by `tests/test_coordination.py`. First set: T1–T12, 60 pairs. Second set: T13–T16, one note type each (staffing limit · a discharge that will not happen today · a bed after the morning round · a note that looks unimportant but took the free bed). Answer mix over all 104 pairs: 27 place in the target ward · 47 temporary placement · 17 wait · 13 escalate — balanced on purpose, so always sending to level 2 does not score well) T17–T19, the ping-pong patterns (below), and T20–T22, the shift rule; 3 pairs each, 30 in all, records reused (`data/answer_key/pairs_set2.yaml`).
- Demand comes **only from the emergency department**. Other demand appears only as beds already held at the start of a template. **Transfers between wards are not simulated.**
- **Case mode** (A56): load a template → the decider decides once → stop and compare. The clock does not move. A decision is: place · temporary placement ("awaiting transfer") · wait · escalate to the shift manager.
- **Why a patient waits** (2026-10-07), in the decision contract: `cleaning` (a bed in cleaning, in the report) · `discharge_today` (a discharge in the report) · `ward_note` (a bed that frees soon by what the ward said in the negotiation — not in the report).
- **12 ward-state templates** in `data/templates/` (T1–T12), relative to the target ward's ladder or with fixed wards.
- **Escalation in a scripted day**: the patient leaves the queue — the shift manager takes over and the simulation no longer follows them. The escalation is recorded (time, reason, flags) for the metrics.
- **Placement in a scripted day**: the bed is held at once; the shift manager's approval arrives 15 minutes later (always approves — no approval screen yet).
- **Scripted days** (A48, A53; built 2026-10-07): busy weekday, Monday 08:00-20:00, 15 admissions (replaced the old 5-patient day, kept as the engine-test fixture `tests/fixtures/small_day.yaml`) · evening and night, Thursday 18:00 to Friday 08:00, 12 admissions · weekend, Friday 14:00 to Saturday 22:00, 12 admissions. Patients reuse the records. Ward notes start and end at set times; a discharge may be `in_report: false` (freed, not updated — only the ward's note tells); the day of the week moves on after midnight. Days have no expected answers: they measure hard-rule violations, time to a bed and escalations.

## Patient: record, severity, needs

- The record is **frozen at the admission decision**; no deterioration trigger.
- Record contract (A61): structured fields + physician note (+ optional triage note); vital signs may be empty; a present value must be plausible; **no clock times** (relative times in the text); unknown fields rejected; **no comments** in record files; NEWS2 never written in a record.
- Free text: Hebrew with English medical terms, **written like a real ED note**: no tidy sections, entries added during the stay ("update", "reassessment"), abbreviations and mixed languages, repeated and irrelevant details, small typos, findings out of order. Facts and traps stay exactly as planned. Trap 7 (noise) buries the key finding deeper than the usual mess.
- **NEWS2** per the Royal College of Physicians 2017 charts (verified, `docs/design/sources.md` S4), computed in code. The Patient Agent may only raise the level, with a quote.
- **Severity judgment notes** (2026-10-07; the list of situations and how the Coordinator uses a note — still open): in clear cases the Patient Agent sets the level by NEWS2 (and may raise it, with a quote). In situations that need clinical judgment (e.g. a limit on treatment — "not a candidate for intensive care") it **never lowers the level**: it keeps the level and adds a **note to the Coordinator**, with a quote, that in effect argues for a lower level. **Approved situations (6, two of each kind):** raise — a treatment that needs intensive care (vasopressor, BiPAP) · a deteriorating trend between measurements; keep the level and add a note — a limit on treatment (not a candidate for intensive care) · confusion that is not new (known dementia — to be checked against the NEWS2 source before use); which data counts — a faulty measurement repeated · two physicians who disagree (the senior's later note counts).- NEWS2 → level (A59, thresholds in `config/clinical_rules/news2_levels.yaml`, read by `news2.py`): **7+ → the system recommends intensive care, for a physician's approval** · 3 points in one parameter → observation · 5–6 → observation · 0–4 → regular unless an observation condition applies. A missing vital sign is never assumed normal.
- Observation conditions (A57): 16 in `config/clinical_rules/observation_conditions.yaml`; NEWS2 **or** the list is enough; supplemental oxygen in both on purpose.
- Isolation conditions (A58): 9 in `config/clinical_rules/isolation_conditions.yaml`, including severe immunosuppression (protective isolation). **Not isolation:** MRSA carriage (contact precautions) · treated past TB · a sick family member when the patient has no symptoms · the physician wrote there is no reason.
- Intensive care: a physician's request is **binding**; NEWS2 7+ is a recommendation for a physician's approval; full → escalate. A patient who does not need intensive care never goes there.
- Chief complaints (A60): closed list of 11 in `config/clinical_rules/chief_complaints.yaml`, with complaint → ward for the baseline.

## Placement

The rules below are encoded in `config/clinical_rules/placement_rules.yaml`.

- **Target ward** (A62), three wordings (written in Hebrew in the records), about a third each: "for admission" only → the agent classifies the ward (required for challenge 3) · "for admission to internal medicine" → that specialty is the target ward, ladder level 1, never changed by the agent; no room → alternatives by the ladder, temporary ones included · "admit to cardiology, discussed with the on-call physician" → binding request in `physician_request.ward`. A family's wish or a consultant's advice is **not** a physician request.
- **Ladders per target ward** (`config/clinical_rules/proximity_ladder.yaml`, approved). Two wards on the same level → **lower occupancy first** (occupied + held + cleaning out of all beds).
- **The shift and the weekend are part of the ward state** (2026-10-07, approved): every ward report shows the shift (morning 07:00-15:00 · evening 15:00-23:00 · night 23:00-07:00), whether it is the weekend (Friday 14:00 to Sunday 07:00), and **on each expected discharge, `likely_within_hour`** — the rule itself, so it is part of the ward state. **A discharge the report shows is waited for only in a weekday morning shift;** in the evening, at night and at the weekend a non-severe patient waits only if the ward itself said the bed frees soon (`ward_note`). In `placement_rules.yaml`; computed in `environment/shifts.py`. Tested by T20 (night), T21 (weekend, the ward says otherwise) and T22 (weekday morning, the discharge will not happen); T14 moved to Wednesday 11:00 so its note still decides.
- **Severe** (needs observation or intensive care) **never waits** — not even for a bed in cleaning; next ladder level at once. **Non-severe** waits up to 1 hour in the target ward, then the next level.
- Temporary placement with "awaiting transfer" applies to any target ward.
- A non-severe patient is **never placed in an observation bed**. An observation-only patient may use an isolation room **only as a last resort** (no observation bed on the whole ladder), with approval. Isolation and observation together → the isolation room.
- **The approver is the shift manager**: every placement; level 3 or farther needs a typed reason. If the user writes "physician" about these approvals, it is a slip — keep the shift manager unless she says explicitly that it changes. (The only physician approval: the NEWS2 7+ intensive-care recommendation.)
- No safe option → escalate, never compromise. Ranking is done in code (`rank_placements`), not by a language model.

## Agents and safety

- **Two agents and a Coordinator** (2026-10-10, approved; the interfaces are built, the agents are entry points only — they are built after each one's code and system prompt is gone over against the course's checklist, and nothing of them goes to Git before that): **Patient Agent** — reads the record once and returns everything about the patient: target ward, binding physician request, level of care, bed type (observation / isolation / intensive care), flags, the judgment note, every finding with a quote · **Department Agent**, one per ward — what its ward can really give · **Coordinator** — an agent with tools that integrates the event and decides where the patient goes; it decides the next step, the code keeps the limits. **Each sees what the others do not:** the Patient Agent the record, each Department Agent its ward's private notes, the Coordinator every ward's report and the two agents' answers — **never the record itself**. Earlier designs (seven, then six agent types: separate Clinical Problem, Severity, Supervision, Isolation and Safety Critic agents) were merged: the patient-side agents read the same record with the same tools and depended on each other.
- **No handoff between agents — everything goes through the Coordinator** (2026-10-11): the Patient Agent and the Department Agents never call each other and never pass anything to each other. Only the Coordinator calls an agent (`assess_patient`, `ask_ward`), gets its answer, and decides what goes on: a Department Agent gets the needs only, from the Coordinator, never the Patient Agent's answer itself. Enforced in code by the permissions (`tools/toolbox.py`): the Patient Agent has only the record tools, a Department Agent only its own ward's view.
- **No Safety Critic agent — its checks are code** (2026-10-10): the hard rules and the flags in the `decide` tool (`tools/decide.py`: a decision that breaks a rule is refused with the reasons; at most 3 refusals in a case, then only an escalation is left) · every quote checked against the record in `assess_patient` (each finding marked `grounded` or not). The assessment is kept inside the toolbox, so the Coordinator cannot change what the Patient Agent found, and cannot decide before the patient is assessed. An independent second reading of the record is given up; the Patient Agent's own measurement shows how often it misreads.
- **Coordinator and Department Agents** (2026-10-07): the **Coordinator sees every ward's report** (free beds, cleaning, discharges today) and uses it to choose where to ask. The **Department Agent of that ward has the final word on its ward**: it accepts, declines with a reason, or offers a condition — from the report **and** what only it knows (its ward notes, e.g. staffing). A decline sends the Coordinator to the next option on the ladder; this back-and-forth is the negotiation ("ping-pong"). Order: Coordinator proposes ward and bed → Department Agent accepts / declines / conditions → the hard rules are checked → hold → shift manager's approval.
- **The Coordinator integrates the case** (2026-10-07, updated 2026-10-10): it decides which ward and bed fit from everything together — the Patient Agent's assessment (target ward, level, bed type, flags, the judgment note) and what the wards answer. It handles what is not known in advance: an offer worth waiting for, a condition, an offer below the need (refused), second-hand information about another ward (ask that ward), an answer that changes with a more exact request. A judgment note is weighed with the rest: e.g. "not a candidate for intensive care" with an intensive-care level → recommend an observation bed, and show the shift manager both the level and the note. Hard rules and ranking stay in code (`decide`, `rank_placements`).
- **Ping-pong patterns and limit** (2026-10-07, approved): three negotiation patterns, one template each in the second set (T17, T18, T19 — built) — **an unsafe offer** (e.g. "no observation bed, but a regular bed with a portable monitor": the Coordinator must decline it and go on) · **second-hand information** ("we heard surgery has room": ask that ward, never rely on it) · **a condition that changes with a more exact request** (first "no"; told only a regular bed is needed: "then yes"). Limit: **3 requests per ward, 8 per case, then escalate** (`placement_rules.yaml`, `negotiation`).
- **No bed that meets the need** (2026-10-10; replaces "back to Supervision"): the need is never lowered. The isolation room as a last resort for an observation-only patient is a rule in `rank_placements` (with the shift manager's approval); when no ward gives a bed that meets the need, the case is escalated.
- **Agent interfaces** (2026-10-10; `docs/design/agent_interfaces.md`): the toolbox with permissions in code (`tools/toolbox.py`) — patient: `read_patient_record`, `compute_news2`, `clinical_lists` · department: `department_view`, bound to one ward, never a record · coordinator: `assess_patient`, `all_department_status`, `rank_placements`, `ask_ward`, `hold_bed`, `release_hold`, `decide`, `escalate_to_human` — never the record · the negotiation contract and channel (`WardRequest` needs only, `WardAnswer` accept / condition / decline; limits 3 and 8 → `LimitReached`) · the placement case, moved by the Coordinator's tools themselves so no step can be skipped: patient assessed → reports → ranked → wards asked → rules checked → decided (`orchestration/placement_case.py`) · the trace with claims and quotes (`observability/tracing.py`) · the Coordinator's flow in code, run on the whole evaluation in the tests (`orchestration/example_pipeline.py`).
- **The decider's time is measured, not assumed** (2026-10-07, approved): in a scripted day the engine measures how long each decision really took and adds it, rounded up to whole minutes (under a second is instant), before the shift manager's approval — so a slow system pays for it in the patients' time. The Code Coordinator adds 5 minutes per phone call (`phone_call_minutes`). The earlier proposal of a fixed minute per request to a ward is dropped.
- **Minutes saved for each patient** (2026-10-07, approved): the time measure the system is judged by — the same day, the same patient, from the admission decision to a bed, against the baseline (`metrics.minutes_saved`; mean and median over patients placed in both; the others listed: placed only by one, escalated, still waiting). Read together with safety: a decider that never asks the wards is faster and less safe.
- **Each agent is built, connected and measured on its own** (2026-10-07, updated 2026-10-10): an agent plugs into the environment through the existing interfaces. Two measures, no change to the scripts: **the agent alone** — `scripts/run_agent_metrics.py`, against the part of the expected answers that is its job (Patient: target ward, binding request, severe, intensive care, observation, isolation, the three flags · Department: a bed now or not as the ward really is, the requests the notes decide), each with a code example to beat (`orchestration/example_agents.py`); `--cases` / `--limit` try a few first; **the whole decision** — `scripts/run_metrics.py --decider module:function`, against the baseline. The system is judged by its final decision.
- **The answers are in the repository; the agents never see them** (2026-10-07): the expected answers are there so each agent can be measured and its mistakes read (no development / test split). The rule: nothing of the answers reaches an agent — not in code (a test guards it) and not in a prompt: prompts are written from the role and `DECISIONS.md`, with no evaluation record, case id or template as an example, and not tuned to single cases (`docs/design/agent_interfaces.md`, section 7).
- **Internal Medicine A or B** (2026-10-08, approved): when the record names neither (only "internal medicine", or only "for admission" with an internal problem), the Patient Agent answers `internal_medicine` and the **Coordinator chooses the one with the lower occupancy** (`rank_placements.resolve_target_ward`; A on a tie) — only it sees the reports. A record that names A or B (a physician's request, or the text) must get that ward. The Patient Agent's measurement counts `internal_medicine`, A or B as right when the record names neither. A patient who needs intensive care is meant for intensive care: that is the target ward.
- **Fixed entry points** (2026-10-07, updated 2026-10-10): every agent file has one function `run`, with the signature it is measured through — Patient `run(toolbox, patient_id) -> PatientAssessment` · Department `run(view, request) -> WardAnswer` · Coordinator `run(engine, event, waiting) -> list[Decision]`, the whole system as one decider. A test guards every signature (`tests/test_agent_entry_points.py`). The Coordinator's `run` hands every case's trace to the measurement with `tracing.collect(trace)`.
- **The measurement output: the goal in one table** (2026-10-08, approved): every report shows the system's goal — **safe** (hard-rule violations, needs met, a bed the ward cannot give), **suitable** (match), **fast** (minutes to a bed over the three days) and, once a decider keeps traces, **tracked** (complete traces, groundedness) — next to today's procedure (the simulated coordinator) and the targets, with ✓ / ✗ (`evaluation/report.py`). `run_metrics.py`: the decider vs today; `run_baselines.py`: the three baselines side by side; `run_agent_metrics.py`: the agent vs its code example (✓ better, = the same, ✗ worse). Everything else — by rule, challenge, trap, template, each day, minutes saved, cost, the wrong cases — only with `--details`, and always in the saved files. A baseline that leaves patients waiting is named under the table, not hidden in its average.
- **The single agent** (2026-10-07; to be built — confirmed 2026-10-10): for the comparison it gets the same tools as the system — role `single_agent` in `tools/toolbox.py`: the record, NEWS2 and the lists, every ward's report, the ranking, the wards through the same negotiation (`ask_ward`), `decide` — never a ward's notes directly — so it compares the architecture, not access to information.
- **Run history — two CSV files that accumulate every run** (2026-10-08, approved and built; replaces the admissions-log plan of 2026-10-07): `runs.csv`, one row per run — the whole system (`run_metrics.py`) or one agent alone (`run_agent_metrics.py`): decider, agent, version, a label saying what changed, and the run's numbers — answers *did it improve?*; `admissions.csv`, one row per patient per run of the whole system — source (pair, paired case or day), target ward, action, ward, bed, bed type, ladder level, temporary, flags, reason, the trace file; for pairs the expected answer, match, violations, needs met; for days the admission decision time, bed time, minutes to a bed, the decider's minutes, the shift manager's approval, escalation time and reason — answers *what changed?*. The version is a fingerprint of `src/`, `config/` and `data/`, never typed: a rerun at the same version replaces its rows, a new version adds rows. Traces saved as `traces/<run id>.jsonl`. Location: `outputs/` locally, a Google Drive folder in Colab (`ED_OUTPUTS`); never in Git. Code: `database/run_log.py`. No database — CSV is simpler.
- **Every decision names the target ward** (2026-10-08, approved): `Decision.target_ward` — the patient's target ward as the decider understood it, for every action; it must agree with the placement (a place is in it, a temporary placement awaits transfer to it). Only "first free bed", which never reads the patient, leaves it empty.
- Agent failure → one retry, then escalate; never guess. Per-case budget. Department Agent gets needs only, no identifiers.
- Text that tries to change system behaviour is ignored and flagged; a physician's clinical order is never treated as injection.
- Permissions enforced in tool code. One sandbox; separate context and tools per agent. Observability from the first step. **Never send `temperature`.**

## Evaluation

- **60 fixed pairs**: 12 templates × 5 records, no random draw — listed in `data/answer_key/pairs.yaml` with the true target ward. Records are written by patient type, target ward, challenge and traps — **without looking at the template**. Plan: `docs/design/ward_state_templates.md`, "The plan of the 60 cases".
- Patient types (true type, from the answer): A non-severe · B severe, observation · C isolation · D isolation + observation · E intensive care. Mix: A 21, B 19, C 7, D 8, E 5.

- **12 text traps**: negation · uncertainty · past vs present · another person · abbreviations · field vs text contradiction · noise · request that is not an order · injection · missing value · complex negation ("cannot be ruled out") · empty field with the value in the text. - **Two patients, one bed** (2026-10-07, approved): **a patient who needs the bed type comes before one for whom it is only a last resort** (an isolation room: the isolation patient before an observation-only patient); then severe before non-severe; then the earlier admission decision. The other patient gets the next option by the usual rules (next ladder level, wait or escalate). In `placement_rules.yaml`, `competing_for_one_bed`.
- **Gold standard ≠ baseline**: the expected answer is the ruler; the baseline is a competitor measured with it.
- **The baseline's code** (2026-10-07, one baseline since 2026-10-10): `tools/rank_placements.py` (on the reports only), `baselines/reading.py` (structured fields), `baselines/code_coordinator.py` (phones the wards along the ladder, 5 minutes a call, `Decision.handling_minutes`; the scripted day adds it before approval). - **Structured signs for the baseline** (2026-10-07, approved, checked against sources S4, S6–S9): in `config/clinical_rules/structured_signs.yaml` — chest pain with troponin >14 ng/L · supplemental oxygen · glucose ≥200 with pH <7.30 · potassium ≥6.0 · glucose <70 · lactate >2 with temperature ≥38.1 · gastrointestinal bleeding · consciousness not alert; plus the NEWS2 levels. COPD with respiratory acidosis is left out (no verified source). Not readable without the text: conditions 2, 3, 6, 12, 13 and every isolation condition.
- **What "improves" means — measured against the baseline** (2026-10-07, approved): the goal is the safest, most suitable bed in the shortest time, so the system is compared with **the baseline — the Code Coordinator, today's procedure written as code — on all three**: safe (hard-rule violations, needs met, a bed the ward cannot give), suitable (match) and fast (time to a bed, in the scripted days), on the same cases and days; plus the charter's absolute targets (0 violations, ≥ 85% match, 100% needs met). One table: the baseline · the system · the target.
- **One baseline — the Code Coordinator** (A49; named and made the only baseline 2026-10-10): today's procedure, written as code, with no agents. It works like a busy shift manager — reads the structured fields and the ward the physician wrote, sees every ward's report, **phones the wards along the ladder (5 minutes a call) and gets their real answer** — a bed that can take this patient, or none — then decides by the same rules and waits 15 minutes for the shift manager's approval. It does not read free text and does not understand a ward's conditions; that is what the agents add. It is also the course's "works without agents". For the phone answer, the true ward state (what each note means for the beds) is in the templates and scenarios, written once, and the note effects are derived from there. Its 4 open points (approved 2026-10-07): no ward written → by the chief complaint · intensive care requested and full → escalate · non-severe waited an hour and no bed on the ladder → escalate · isolation → not known (no structured field). · The "first free bed" and "rules only" baselines were removed (2026-10-10): three baselines made the comparison hard to read. · **Single agent** comparison for 30.10 (A50).
- Time to a bed is measured only in the scripted days; case mode measures the system's response time. The old charter target "≥ 20% faster than first free bed" is dropped.
- **The measurement, as in the charter** (2026-10-07; the charter updated 2026-10-10): the system is measured by its final placement against the expected answer, next to the baseline (the Code Coordinator) — **safe:** 0 hard-rule violations · 100% of observation / isolation needs met or escalated · 0 placements in a bed the ward cannot give — **suitable:** ≥ 85% match — **fast:** fewer minutes to a bed than the baseline — **tracked:** 100% complete traces · ≥ 95% groundedness. The charter's two targets relative to "first free bed" (off-service ≤ half, 20% faster) were replaced: no hospital uses first free bed, and it leaves patients waiting. Each agent is also measured alone, against its part of the answers. The single agent is measured the same way.
- **Metrics code** (built 2026-10-07, `evaluation/metrics.py`, run by `scripts/run_metrics.py`): per decision — match (action; for a placement, an acceptable ward and the bed type; for a wait, the ward), **match from the reports alone** (2026-10-07, approved: the same comparison with the answer a decider would give reading the patient right but never asking the wards — reachable without Department Agents; the gap between the two is what they add), hard-rule violations (6 rules), a bed the ward cannot really give, need met (by the bed, by escalation, or a non-severe patient waiting), off-service, missing flags and physician approval; counted overall and by template, trap, challenge and patient type, against the charter's targets. Paired cases score each patient in the hospital as it is after the other took its bed. Scripted days: violations, time to a bed (all and severe), escalations, still waiting.
- Report results per template and per trap, not only overall.
- Example files mirror the real structure exactly — no comments or notes inside.
