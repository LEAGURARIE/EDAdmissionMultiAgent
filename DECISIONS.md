## Hospital and environment

- Wards: Internal Medicine A, Internal Medicine B, Cardiology, General Surgery — 10 regular + 2 observation + 1 isolation room each; Intensive Care — 6 + 1 isolation room. Intermediate care = the observation beds.
- Bed states: free → held → occupied → cleaning → free; held → free when the hold expires. Cleaning always 45 minutes. A hold expires after 30 minutes (both in `config/hospital.yaml`, with the shift manager's approval time — 15 minutes); two cases can never hold the same bed.
- **Isolation room has observation capability** and is reserved for isolation patients (A51).
- The simulation engine is the only code that changes hospital state. **The environment does not order the queue** — the decider does (A47). **The environment never reveals needs or answers**: scenarios and the queue hold only patient id and decision time.
- Ward report (A55): counts by bed type and state · free beds by number · beds in cleaning with the time they will be ready · **discharges expected today, without the hour** · held beds count as unavailable, without saying for whom or until when · the day of the week · no patient identifiers.
- **Ward notes** (2026-10-07): every template gives wards private notes — what only that ward knows (staffing, a broken bed or room, a bed promised to someone, a bed freed but not yet updated, a discharge that will not happen, a long cleaning, a ward that takes only its own patients tonight). They are kept on the ward, never in the ward report, and only that ward's Department Agent reads them (`engine.ward_notes`). What each note means for the beds is written next to it in the template or scenario (`note_effects`, applied by `environment/ward_truth.py`) — the true ward state lives in the environment, once; the expected answers and the simulated coordinator's phone call both use it.
- **Every pair tests both reading and coordination** (2026-10-07): the records test reading (challenges, traps); the notes make the ward reports alone lead to a wrong answer in **every pair** — guarded by `tests/test_coordination.py`. First set: T1–T12, 60 pairs. Second set: T13–T16, one note type each (staffing limit · a discharge that will not happen today · a bed after the morning round · a note that looks unimportant but took the free bed). Answer mix over all 104 pairs: 27 place in the target ward · 47 temporary placement · 17 wait · 13 escalate — balanced on purpose, so always sending to level 2 does not score well) T17–T19, the ping-pong patterns (below), and T20–T22, the shift rule; 3 pairs each, 30 in all, records reused (`data/answer_key/pairs_set2.yaml`).
- Demand comes **only from the emergency department**. Other demand appears only as beds already held at the start of a template. **Transfers between wards are not simulated.**
- **Case mode** (A56): load a template → the decider decides once → stop and compare. The clock does not move. A decision is: place · temporary placement ("awaiting transfer") · wait · escalate to the shift manager.
- **Why a patient waits** (2026-10-07), in the decision contract: `cleaning` (a bed in cleaning, in the report) · `discharge_today` (a discharge in the report) · `ward_note` (a bed that frees soon by what the ward said in the negotiation — not in the report).
- **12 ward-state templates** in `data/templates/` (T1–T12), relative to the target ward's ladder or with fixed wards.
- **Escalation in a scripted day**: the patient leaves the queue — the shift manager takes over and the simulation no longer follows them. The escalation is recorded (time, reason, flags) for the metrics.
- **Placement in a scripted day**: the bed is held when the case closes; the shift manager's approval arrives 15 minutes later (always approves — no approval screen yet).
- **The case desk** (2026-10-14, Lea's design): in a scripted day every decision is a case. **A patient case** opens at an admission decision — the decider gets that one patient. **A bed case** opens when a bed frees (or a hold expires, or an approval falls through) while patients wait — the decider gets every patient waiting then and decides who gets what. **No hint:** the decider gets only ids and admission decision times; who is severe it must find out from the records (`tests/test_no_hints.py`). **Cases at once** (`concurrent_cases` in `config/hospital.yaml`, default 1 — one shift manager; a day may set its own; more wait in a queue, in the order they came). **The clock goes on while a case is open:** it closes after the time the decider really took. **The bed is held when the case closes** (Lea's choice): two open cases may choose the same bed — the later one finds it taken (a conflict) and that patient gets a new case. **A bed that was not free when the case opened is the decider's error, not a conflict:** one more case, then escalation. **A day that contradicts itself never runs** (`environment/scenario_check.py`, checked on load and by `tests/test_scenario_check.py`): every ward and bed it names exists; a discharge only frees a bed an inpatient holds — never a bed free at the start, never the same bed twice; a bed freed without updating the system is told by a note of that ward; a note names only beds of its ward and ends only after it started; every admitted patient has a record and is admitted once. Measured per day: cases, bed cases, minutes in the queue, minutes per case, most cases open at once, conflicts, bed errors.
- **Patients waiting together are handed together** (2026-10-14, approved): patients waiting for a case at the same moment — admitted in the same minute, or piled up while the desk was busy — come to the decider in one case, side by side, the way a shift manager sees the list; the decider decides who comes first.
- **Day 4 — competing for a bed** (2026-10-14, approved; `data/scenarios/competing_for_a_bed.yaml`): three moments when two patients need one isolation room — **the same state, the earlier decision first** (P-046, P-058; the waiting patient keeps the bed it waited for) · **the bed type needed before a last resort** (P-030 observation only, P-043 isolation) · **severe before non-severe** (P-050, P-057). The moments are listed in `data/answer_key/contention_moments.yaml` without the answer: **who should get the bed is computed at the moment** from the patients' true needs and the approved rule, and a moment the day did not reach as designed — the patients not together, the bed not free, another bed of that type free — is reported apart, never scored (`metrics.contention_moments`). Shown apart from the three days: not in the minutes to a bed — one of the two patients waits by design, and a system that misses the isolation need does not wait, so it would look faster for being less safe. The comparison report (2026-11-09, Lea approved) shows the three days day by day and pooled over every patient placed, and day 4 apart: its moments decided right and its violations, for both systems and the baseline (`comparison_report.py`; protocol, section 9).
- **Scripted days** (A48, A53; built 2026-10-07; day 4 — competing for a bed — added 2026-10-14, see above): busy weekday, Monday 08:00-20:00, 15 admissions (replaced the old 5-patient day, kept as the engine-test fixture `tests/fixtures/small_day.yaml`) · evening and night, Thursday 18:00 to Friday 08:00, 12 admissions · weekend, Friday 14:00 to Saturday 22:00, 12 admissions. Patients reuse the records. Ward notes start and end at set times; a discharge may be `in_report: false` (freed, not updated — only the ward's note tells); the day of the week moves on after midnight. Days have no expected answers: they measure hard-rule violations, time to a bed and escalations.

## Patient: record, severity, needs

- The record is **frozen at the admission decision**; no deterioration trigger.
- Record contract (A61): structured fields + physician note (+ optional triage note); vital signs may be empty; a present value must be plausible; **no clock times** (relative times in the text); unknown fields rejected; **no comments** in record files; NEWS2 never written in a record.
- Free text: Hebrew with English medical terms, **written like a real ED note**: no tidy sections, entries added during the stay ("update", "reassessment"), abbreviations and mixed languages, repeated and irrelevant details, small typos, findings out of order. Facts and traps stay exactly as planned. Trap 7 (noise) buries the key finding deeper than the usual mess.
- **NEWS2** per the Royal College of Physicians 2017 charts (verified, `docs/design/sources.md` S4), computed in code. The Patient Agent may only raise the level, with a quote. **A missing vital sign is never assumed normal:** NEWS2 decides only the level the known points already prove, and the missing sign is a `missing_vital_sign` flag. **No vital sign at all → escalate** (Lea, 2026-11-08): a patient whose record has no vital sign is neither placed nor kept waiting — the hard-rule check in `decide` refuses it, and the patient goes to the shift manager. It holds for both systems; no record in the data has it, so no result measured so far changes.
- **Severity judgment notes** (2026-10-07; how a note is used updated 2026-11-08: shown to the shift manager on the proposal, weighed by the Judge Agent — rubric item 9): in clear cases the Patient Agent sets the level by NEWS2 (and may raise it, with a quote). In situations that need clinical judgment (e.g. a limit on treatment — "not a candidate for intensive care") it **never lowers the level**: it keeps the level and adds a **note**, with a quote, that in effect argues for a lower level. **Approved situations (6, two of each kind):** raise — a treatment that needs intensive care (vasopressor, BiPAP) · a deteriorating trend between measurements; keep the level and add a note — a limit on treatment (not a candidate for intensive care) · confusion that is not new (known dementia — to be checked against the NEWS2 source before use; **a known contradiction, kept on purpose 2026-10-15**: the NEWS2 source (sources.md S5) and the answer key do not count it, while this line and the single agent's prompt say keep the level — Lea: leave it as it is, give the multi-agent system the same rule, and see how it does; both systems are measured under it, and the report says so); which data counts — a faulty measurement repeated · two physicians who disagree (the senior's later note counts). **In `config/clinical_rules/severity_judgment.yaml` since 2026-10-15, handed to the agents of both systems by `clinical_lists`** (Lea; before, they reached no agent); a situation not yet approved is never handed over.- NEWS2 → level (A59, thresholds in `config/clinical_rules/news2_levels.yaml`, read by `news2.py`): **7+ → the system recommends intensive care, for a physician's approval** · 3 points in one parameter → observation · 5–6 → observation · 0–4 → regular unless an observation condition applies. A missing vital sign is never assumed normal.
- Observation conditions (A57): 16 in `config/clinical_rules/observation_conditions.yaml`; NEWS2 **or** the list is enough; supplemental oxygen in both on purpose.
- Isolation conditions (A58): 9 in `config/clinical_rules/isolation_conditions.yaml`, including severe immunosuppression (protective isolation). **Not isolation:** MRSA carriage (contact precautions) · treated past TB · a sick family member when the patient has no symptoms · the physician wrote there is no reason.
- Intensive care: a physician's request is **binding**; NEWS2 7+ is a recommendation for a physician's approval; full → escalate. A patient who does not need intensive care never goes there.
- Chief complaints (A60): closed list of 11 in `config/clinical_rules/chief_complaints.yaml`, with complaint → ward for the baseline.

## Placement

The values the code reads — the waiting time, the shifts and the weekend, the ladder level that needs a typed reason, the request limits and the order for two patients and one bed — are in `config/clinical_rules/placement_rules.yaml`. The other rules below are written in the code (`rank_placements`, `decide`).

- **Target ward** (A62), three wordings (written in Hebrew in the records), about a third each: "for admission" only → the agent classifies the ward (required for challenge 3) · "for admission to internal medicine" → that specialty is the target ward, ladder level 1, never changed by the agent; no room → alternatives by the ladder, temporary ones included · "admit to cardiology, discussed with the on-call physician" → binding request in `physician_request.ward`. A family's wish or a consultant's advice is **not** a physician request.
- **Ladders per target ward** (`config/clinical_rules/proximity_ladder.yaml`, approved). Two wards on the same level → **lower occupancy first** (occupied + held + cleaning out of all beds).
- **The shift and the weekend are part of the ward state** (2026-10-07, approved): every ward report shows the shift (morning 07:00-15:00 · evening 15:00-23:00 · night 23:00-07:00), whether it is the weekend (Friday 14:00 to Sunday 07:00), and **on each expected discharge, `likely_within_hour`** — the rule itself, so it is part of the ward state. **A discharge the report shows is waited for only in a weekday morning shift;** in the evening, at night and at the weekend a non-severe patient waits only if the ward itself said the bed frees soon (`ward_note`). In `placement_rules.yaml`; computed in `environment/shifts.py`. Tested by T20 (night), T21 (weekend, the ward says otherwise) and T22 (weekday morning, the discharge will not happen); T14 moved to Wednesday 11:00 so its note still decides.
- **Severe** (needs observation or intensive care) **never waits** — not even for a bed in cleaning; next ladder level at once. **Non-severe** waits up to 1 hour in the target ward, then the next level.
- Temporary placement with "awaiting transfer" applies to any target ward.
- A non-severe patient is **never placed in an observation bed**. An observation-only patient may use an isolation room **only as a last resort** (no observation bed on the whole ladder), with approval. Isolation and observation together → the isolation room. **A note that tells of a bed freed but not yet updated names the bed, and that bed is the free one** (2026-10-15, Lea: a measurement bug fixed — before, the first occupied bed of the type was freed; recorded as a deviation in the comparison protocol; the baseline unchanged). **A ward's note that it cannot take another observation patient (no nurse for its observation beds) does not close its isolation room as that last resort** (Lea, 2026-10-15, after the single agent's trial escalated P-064 on T13 instead; the expected answer stands).
- **The approver is the shift manager**: every placement; level 3 or farther needs a typed reason. If the user writes "physician" about these approvals, it is a slip — keep the shift manager unless she says explicitly that it changes. (The only physician approval: the NEWS2 7+ intensive-care recommendation.)
- No safe option → escalate, never compromise. Ranking is done in code (`rank_placements`), not by a language model.

## Agents and safety

- **An orchestra: a Coordinator and three agents** (2026-10-14; replaces "two agents and a Coordinator" of 2026-10-10). **Coordinator** (code, approved by Lea 2026-11-08; replaces the Coordinator as a model) — the conductor is **code, not an agent**: the LangGraph graph itself, its nodes and the functions that pick the next node. It activates the agents, checks what they return (a handoff check in code after every agent — Pydantic schema with no extra fields, closed values, values that must exist, length limits, closing tags neutralized; after the Patient Agent also every quote found in the record), and decides the next step by the state of the case — **routing reads closed fields only**, never free text. It makes no model call, so it cannot overrule, and adds no routing cost; the price is less flexibility in a case the code did not foresee, which ends in escalation. **Patient Agent** — the only one that reads the record: a full assessment (target ward, binding physician request, level of care, bed type, flags, the judgment note, every finding with a quote), and answers to targeted questions the Coordinator passes on. **Department Agent** (rung by rung, approved by Lea 2026-11-08; replaces "one ward at a time") — the same agent, code and prompt, run once for each ward on the **current rung** of the ranking, the wards of one rung in parallel; the next rung is asked only when no ward on this rung gives a bed. Each run gets the patient's needs only and sees one ward's report and private notes; nothing carries from ward to ward. It answers accept, decline with a reason, or a condition, and has the final word on its ward; code picks the highest-ranked ward that accepted. Parallel calls are capped for the model provider's rate limit, and a retry never reserves a bed twice. **A ward's question about the patient** (approved by Lea 2026-11-08): a ward that takes the patient only on a condition about the patient (today: stable) says so in a closed field (`asks`), never in free text; **code answers it from the assessment, with no model call** (Lea, 2026-11-08; replaces the Patient Agent's `PatientAnswer`): **stable means not severe** — no observation, no intensive care, not severe — the same reading as the expected answers (T19); yes or no, no third value. The Department Agent is run again for that ward only, with the answer. A model's second reading could contradict the assessment the rest of the case rests on. **Where the ward knows a bed is free** (Lea, 2026-11-08): every bed a ward gives comes with a closed `source` — `report` (free, in cleaning or a discharge today in its report) or `note` (only its staff's notes say so). Code checks it at the handoff: a bed "from the report" that the report does not show coming free, or a bed "from a note" in a ward with no note, goes back to the Department Agent; a bed from a note is marked for the shift manager — the system has not caught up with it. Whether the note really speaks of that bed needs language: the environment catches it when the case closes. One question per ward per case; without an answer the ward's answer stands as a condition. **Judge** — checks a proposed placement against a rubric before it becomes a decision. **Each sees what the others do not:** the record only the Patient Agent, a ward's notes only the Department Agent while it looks at that ward, the Coordinator the agents' answers. The agents are built after each one's code and system prompt is gone over together, and nothing of them goes to Git before that. Earlier designs (seven, then six agent types, then two agents and a Coordinator with tools) were merged or replaced.
- **What is not known in advance** (2026-10-07; updated 2026-11-08, the Coordinator is code): the graph picks the next node from closed fields only — a ward's answer (accept · condition · decline), its closed question about the patient, a check passed or not, the Judge Agent's valid or not. An offer worth waiting for is a condition with its minutes; a condition about the patient goes to the Patient Agent; a decline sends the search to the next ward or rung. A case the code did not foresee ends in escalation, never in a guess. A judgment note is shown to the shift manager with the level and weighed by the Judge Agent (rubric item 9). The ranking and the hard rules stay in code.
- **No handoff between agents — everything goes through the graph** (2026-10-11, kept 2026-10-14; updated 2026-11-08): the agents never call each other and never pass anything to each other. The graph runs all three, checks each answer in code and decides what goes on: a Department Agent run gets the needs only, never the Patient Agent's answer itself. A ward that needs something about the patient asks in a closed field, and code answers from the assessment; the Judge Agent reads the case's trace, never the record, and asks nothing. Enforced in code by the permissions (`tools/toolbox.py`), the contracts and tests.
- **The Judge and its rubric** (2026-10-14; approved by Lea 2026-10-17, item 11 added; `config/judge_rubric.yaml`): every item is pass / fail with a one-line reason. **Blocking:** (1) the hard rules — level of care, observation → observation bed, isolation → isolation room, intensive care only for who needs it, a severe patient never waits · (2) every need is backed by the assessment's findings and quotes — negation, uncertainty, "cannot be ruled out" — and none is left out where a quoted sentence shows it (2026-11-09, Lea) · (3) no flag dropped · (4) the target ward kept; elsewhere is a temporary placement awaiting transfer · (5) the ladder order — no closer ward with a suitable bed skipped · (6) the bed was really offered, exists and is of the right type · (7) the waiting rule · (8) the reason is grounded in the wards' answers and the assessment. **Not blocking, shown to the shift manager:** (9) a judgment note weighed · (10) level 3 or farther marked for a typed reason. **An escalation is checked too** (2026-10-17): (11, blocking) an escalation for lack of a bed is valid only when every ward on the ranking that could give a bed meeting the need was looked at, its notes included — a note can free a bed — and the isolation room as a last resort for an observation-only patient was not left unoffered (the single agent's misses of this kind on the development and test sets). **Two layers, code first** (approved by Lea 2026-11-08; replaces the Judge's `check_rules` tool): **layer 1, code** — the items code can check (1, 3–7, 10, 11) run on the proposal's fields and block: a proposal that fails goes back to the ranking (the next option or rung), and at the end of the rounds to escalation with code's reasons; the Judge Agent does not run on it. **Layer 2, the Judge Agent** — runs only on a proposal that passed layer 1, so it can never approve what code blocked. It reads the case's trace up to the proposal, wrapped as data — the findings with their quotes, the wards' answers with their reasons, the results of the code checks; **and, since 2026-11-08 (Lea), the whole sentence of the record each quote was taken from** (a quote can be found in the record and still be cut short — "TB" out of "rule out TB" — and only its sentence shows how it was meant; never the whole record) **and the notes of the wards that answered** (so it can see a bed "from a note" is in the note; never a ward that was not asked) — the tool `read_case`, role judge, built in `agents/orchestra_tools.py` — and checks what code cannot: that every need rests on a quote **read correctly** (negation, uncertainty, "cannot be ruled out" — item 2), that the reason is grounded (8), that a judgment note was weighed (9), and that the wards' notes were understood. It returns a closed contract — valid / not valid, each failed item with a short reason — and only "valid" routes. Not valid → one round of correction with its reasons, then escalation to the shift manager with the proposal and the reasons. **The correction goes to whoever made the mistake, through the graph** (Lea, 2026-11-08): the graph reads the failed item's number — a closed value, never the correction's words — item 2 failed (a need rests on a quote read wrong) → the Patient Agent, who reads the whole record, assesses again with the Judge's correction (`PatientTask.judge_feedback`), and the ranking and the wards follow its new assessment; only item 8 failed → the wards (`WardTask.judge_feedback`). The correction is a handoff like any other: limited in length, tagged as data, checked in code. **Every verdict answers all three of its items** (2, 8, 9) — none can be skipped. The Judge Agent's tools `read_case` and `give_verdict` are built in `agents/orchestra_tools.py`. **The Coordinator's code, part 3a, built 2026-11-08** (`orchestration/handoff.py`, `proposal.py`, `code_checks.py`; `tests/test_coordinator_code.py`): `validate_handoff` after every agent (closed failures; a tool_output tag in free text neutralized, kept); **the wards asked on a rung are every ward on that level of the ladder, not only the ones the report shows a bed in** — a note can free a bed (T19); an observation-only patient's ladder is asked again for the isolation room as a last resort; the proposal and its reason are written by code — the ward highest on the ladder that accepted (same level: lower occupancy), or a wait in the target ward within the hour for a non-severe patient; and layer 1 (items 1, 3–7, 10, 11) — item 1 is the same `decide` check the single agent's decisions pass. **Part 3b, the graph, built 2026-11-08** (`orchestration/graph.py`; `tests/test_coordinator_graph.py`, every route with scripted agents on T19): the wards of a rung in parallel (LangGraph `Send`), the cost added by every run, the state saved after every node. Two rules found while building it: **an offer the code checks blocked does not count against the next option** (item 5 — a ward that offered a bed below the need was not "skipped"), and **the request limits (3 per ward, 8 per case) are counted per assessment** — a new assessment after the Judge's correction asks the wards afresh; the correction round itself stays one. **Part 3c, the decider, built 2026-11-08** (`agents/multi_agent.py`; `tests/test_multi_agent_decider.py`): `run(engine, event, waiting)`, the single agent's contract. Every patient of a case is assessed first (the graph pauses after the Patient Agent), then code orders them by the approved rule for competing patients — written in the decider from the assessment, never imported from the evaluation code — and each graph goes on in that order; a bed given to one patient is not proposed to the next, and does not count against the next's options (item 5). **The case's cost cap covers every patient of the case together.** At most `parallel_agent_calls` agent runs at once (`config/agents.yaml`, 4). The state is saved with a serializer that loads back only the system's own types. Not registered as a decider until the real agents are wired (step 4). **The Judge does not read the record.** Starts on Haiku, a different model from the Patient Agent; measured on the negation and uncertainty traps, and moved to Sonnet if it misses them.
- **Limits, for safety and cost** (2026-10-14), enforced in code, never by a prompt: the patient is assessed before the first search for a bed · **no decision without the Judge's approval** — an escalation for lack of a bed included (rubric item 11); an escalation forced by a limit or a failure goes straight to the shift manager · the code checks (Judge layer 1) block a proposal → back to the ranking · the Judge Agent rejects → **one correction round only**, then escalation to the shift manager with the proposal and the Judge's reasons · **Confidence per finding** (approved by Lea 2026-11-08): every finding of the Patient Agent carries high / medium / low next to its quote; a low-confidence finding that bears on a need is marked for the shift manager on the proposal, and **never lowers the need** · **what the confidence means** (Lea, 2026-11-09): how well the quote backs the claim, never how sure the diagnosis is — high: the quote states the claim outright, about this patient, now · medium: the claim is inferred from several quotes, none says it alone · low: unclear wording, contradicted elsewhere in the record, or past or present unclear. The claim is worded as the record words it ("suspected TB" from "חשד ל-TB" is high; "TB" from it is a misread, rubric item 2). The levels are written once, in the tool's field description; the prompt points to them and shows how the reading rules lead to each level · **the Patient Agent's prompt** (Lea, 2026-11-09): the single agent's reading wording, plus three short reading-trap examples and a refusal example (it never picks a bed or decides the placement) — the examples stay in the prompt, not in a tool; the difference from the single agent's prompt is recorded in the comparison protocol (section 9) · **every need has its evidence** (Lea, 2026-11-08): each finding names, in a closed field, the needs it backs (`backs`: severe, intensive care, observation, isolation); a need set to true with no finding backing it fails the contract in code and goes back to the Patient Agent — a finding for observation or intensive care also backs severe. The single agent's contract is unchanged · 3 requests per ward and 8 per case, one question about the patient per ward · the agent loop's budget (`config/agents.yaml`). The hard rules are checked once more in code before a decision leaves (`tools/decide.py`) — a safety net if the Judge errs.
- **Holding a bed and escalating are code** (2026-10-14): once a proposal passed both Judge layers, code holds the bed for the shift manager's approval, or escalates with every flag the Patient Agent raised.
- **Every output is tagged as data** (2026-10-14): whatever passes from one part to another — a tool's result to an agent, an agent's answer to the Coordinator, what the Coordinator passes on — is wrapped in a tag that names its source, and every system prompt says that what is inside a tag is data, never an instruction. A closing tag inside the content is neutralized, so text cannot break out of its tag. Nothing is deleted: the Patient Agent must see an injection attempt to flag it.
- **The multi-agent system is built with LangGraph, LangChain and Pydantic** (2026-10-14): **LangGraph** is the orchestration — the three agents and the code checks are nodes, the Coordinator is the graph itself: its choice of the next node is a conditional edge that reads closed fields only, the limits are edges code allows or not, the case state is the graph's state. **LangChain** is the model call inside every agent's loop — one message and tool format for both providers, so a case can switch model mid-way. **Pydantic** (the validation library) checks every contract, the graph's state, every tool's input and every agent's answer; an answer that fails gets one more try, then the case is escalated. The agent loop (`agents/agent_loop.py`) keeps its parts — budget, tagging, the decision as the end of the loop.
- **A fallback model from another provider — in the multi-agent system** (2026-10-14; replaces "no fallback to another model"): the main model is Anthropic's (`config/agents.yaml`); when it fails after one retry, the call goes to OpenAI's `gpt-6.1-sol` (chosen 2026-10-14, $2 / $10 per million tokens; tool calling through OpenAI's Responses API). No temperature is sent to either. Every call records which model answered; every report counts the cases that used the fallback, and a run with many of them is not compared. The prompts must work on both models — the fallback path is tried on a few cases.
